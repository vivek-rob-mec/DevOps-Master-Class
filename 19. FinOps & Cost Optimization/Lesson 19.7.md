# Module 19 — FinOps & Cost Optimization

## Lesson 7: Pricing, Commitments and Interruptible Capacity

# 19.7.1 Rate optimization comes after usage

Common levers:

```text
on-demand/list rate
savings plans/reserved commitments
reserved capacity
spot/interruptible
volume/service tier
private pricing/enterprise agreement
license optimization
```

Understand scope, term, payment, flexibility, sharing, coverage, utilization, break-even, and lock-in.

# 19.7.2 Commitment model

Commit only the stable baseline with high confidence.

```text
eligible hourly spend distribution
minus planned shutdown/rightsizing/migration
minus uncertainty reserve
= commitment candidate
```

Monitor:

```text
coverage    eligible usage receiving discount
utilization purchased commitment actually consumed
effective savings after unused commitment
```

High coverage with poor utilization can waste money. High utilization with low coverage may leave safe savings available.

# 19.7.3 Portfolio layering

```text
stable base      → commitment
variable demand  → on-demand
interruptible    → spot
rare peaks       → on-demand/queue/load shedding
```

Diversify instance families/regions only where workload compatibility and risk allow. Track hidden migration/engineering cost.

# 19.7.4 Spot architecture

Use stateless replicated services, batch with checkpoints, CI workers, and queue consumers. Handle interruption, diversify pools, bound retry storms, preserve on-demand fallback, and ensure the database or shared dependency can handle rapid recovery.

# 19.7.5 Lab

Given 90 days of hourly eligible spend, calculate P50/P80/P95 stable baselines. Propose conservative and aggressive commitment portfolios with downside scenarios for rightsizing, migration, and 30% demand decline. Define monthly reforecast and expiration management.

# 19.7.6 Governance

Commitment purchase needs accountable owner, forecast evidence, finance/procurement review, utilization monitoring, expiry calendar, and documented reallocation plan. A discount is a financial obligation, not a free optimization.

# 19.7.7 Beginner mental model: subscription after stable need

A discounted annual train pass is valuable when commuting is stable. It is wasteful when travel stops or the pass covers the wrong route. Cloud commitments exchange flexibility for a lower eligible rate under specific terms.

Remove avoidable usage and understand demand before committing.

# 19.7.8 Rate versus usage optimization

```text
usage optimization: consume fewer/better resources
rate optimization: pay a better effective price for eligible usage
architecture optimization: redesign how value is delivered
```

A commitment can hide rightsizing savings because the invoice remains charged until the commitment is reused or expires. Track utilization and coverage.

# 19.7.9 Commitment vocabulary

```text
coverage: eligible usage receiving committed/discounted rate
utilization: purchased commitment actually applied
term: commitment duration
payment option: timing of payment affecting discount/cash
scope/flexibility: services/regions/instance properties eligible
effective rate: real cost after allocation/fees/benefits
```

Definitions and products vary by provider and can change; consult current official terms before purchasing.

# 19.7.10 Baseline demand

Use a representative lookback and forward plan:

```text
steady always-on usage by eligible category
seasonality and growth/decline
rightsizing/scheduling/migration roadmap
new product and retirement
regional/provider strategy
existing commitments and expiry
failure/recovery reserve
```

Commit only the confidence-adjusted floor, not the most optimistic forecast.

# 19.7.11 Coverage/utilization tradeoff

High commitment increases possible coverage/discount but risks unused commitment. Low commitment preserves flexibility but leaves stable usage at variable/on-demand rates.

Create scenarios:

```text
downside demand
base forecast
upside demand
migration/rightsizing acceleration
regional outage or strategy change
```

Quantify total effective cost and unused commitment in each.

# 19.7.12 Portfolio layering

A risk-balanced portfolio may combine:

```text
committed capacity/rate for stable baseline
on-demand for variable/uncertain workloads
interruptible/Spot for tolerant workloads
scheduled/elastic capacity for known windows
specialized agreements/credits as appropriate
```

Do not treat interruption discounts as a commitment substitute; workload failure behavior differs.

# 19.7.13 Commitment ownership and allocation

Decide who owns benefits and unused cost:

```text
central portfolio shared across business
business/product-specific commitment
account/project-specific scope
benefit distributed by eligible usage
unused amount held centrally or allocated by policy
```

Publish amortized/effective treatment and prevent teams from seeing distorted cost that discourages good usage decisions.

# 19.7.14 Spot/interruptible architecture

Eligible workloads:

```text
stateless horizontally distributed services with stable baseline
batch with checkpoints
idempotent queue workers
CI/test jobs that can retry safely
fault-tolerant data processing
```

Avoid depending entirely on interruptible supply for a critical non-checkpointable singleton or tight deadline without fallback.

# 19.7.15 Interruption economics

```text
effective useful-work cost
= compute charges
+ repeated/failed work
+ checkpoint/storage/transfer
+ fallback capacity
+ engineering/incident cost
```

A low instance price is not low cost if 40% of work repeatedly restarts.

# 19.7.16 Real hands-on commitment model

Use synthetic 12-month eligible hourly spend:

1. Normalize credits and one-time anomalies.
2. Remove approved rightsizing/scheduling forecast.
3. Model downside/base/upside demand.
4. Compare several commitment levels and flexibility.
5. Calculate expected utilization, coverage, unused cost, effective rate.
6. Add expiry ladder and review cadence.
7. Document recommendation, uncertainty, approvers, and exit constraints.

# 19.7.17 Real hands-on Spot test

Run a queue worker on diversified interruptible node types with stable fallback. Inject interruption, measure notice/drain/checkpoint, duplicate/retry, queue age, useful work, replacement capacity, and effective cost. Verify user freshness SLO.

# 19.7.18 Failure scenario: migration after purchase

Three months after a long commitment, the product moves to a different service/region/provider. Assess product flexibility, transfer/exchange options under actual terms, remaining eligible usage, portfolio reallocation, impairment/unused cost, and governance learning.

This is why architecture roadmap belongs in the purchase decision.

# 19.7.19 Governance checklist

- Current official terms/pricing are verified.
- Usage waste and planned changes are accounted for first.
- Forecast includes scenario/confidence, not one point.
- Coverage/utilization/unused cost and expiry are monitored.
- Benefit and unused-cost allocation is published.
- Purchase has finance/procurement/engineering/product approval.
- Concentration/lock-in and exit are assessed.
- Interruptible workloads have tested resilience/effective cost.
- Realized benefit uses comparable effective rates.

# 19.7.20 Certification and interview preparation

Rate optimization is a FinOps capability, but product names/terms change. For exam or purchase decisions, use current official provider and FinOps documentation. This tutorial is educational, not a financial recommendation.

**Beginner: Usage versus rate optimization?**  Use fewer/better resources versus pay a better rate for eligible consumption.

**Intermediate: Coverage versus utilization?**  Coverage is eligible usage discounted; utilization is purchased commitment applied rather than wasted.

**Intermediate: Why commit only stable baseline?**  Commitments reduce flexibility and unused purchase can outweigh discount when usage falls/moves.

**Senior: What data supports purchase?**  Normalized history, rightsizing/scheduling plan, future demand/migrations, existing portfolio/expiry, scenarios, terms, and ownership.

**Senior: How do you calculate Spot value?**  Useful work per total cost including interruptions, retries, checkpoints, fallback, operations, and SLO—not list discount.

**Expert: Who gets commitment discount?**  Follow a published allocation policy aligned to ownership/incentives, reconcile amortized cost/benefit/unused amount, and prevent arbitrary windfalls.

**Architect: How do commitments affect architecture?**  They create economic switching constraints; balance discount with service/region/provider flexibility, roadmap uncertainty, resilience, and exit plan.

**Never-forget answer:** commitments price stable confidence. Optimize usage first, model downside, layer the portfolio, and monitor utilization, coverage, and flexibility continuously.
